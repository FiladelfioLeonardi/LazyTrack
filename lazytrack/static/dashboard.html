        📖 How Mochi works

        Mochi tracks your day by itself (screen time, coding, commits, the webcam for smoking) and lets you log the rest with one tap. Your pet Mochi lives on how you do: keep it alive as long as you can.

        Getting started

        1. Install. Windows: double-click Mochi-Setup.exe (if Windows warns: More info → Run anyway). Mac: unzip Install-Mochi-Mac.zip, right-click Install Mochi → Open. It installs everything by itself, no Python needed.
        2. Start. Mochi is in the Start menu / desktop (Windows) or Applications (Mac), starts with the computer and runs in the background. Mochi opens in its own app window. Closing the window doesn't stop it: it keeps tracking in the background, and the shortcut brings the window back. (It also works in any browser at localhost:8042, which is how the phone uses it.)
        3. Make your account: name, username, email and password. The same account logs in on your other devices.
        4. Setup: pick what you want to do less of and more of (or add your own), what to show from your screen, extras (42, GitHub), then set your targets.

        Right after the setup a tutorial walks you through the app. Skip any step or the whole thing; the 🧭 Tutorial button at the top brings it back any time.

        Mochi has to be running to track; the installer makes it start with the computer. To remove it: Windows Settings → Apps → Mochi, or "Uninstall Mochi" in your Mac's Applications folder. Your data stays unless you choose to delete it.

        HP, life and death

        Mochi has HP from 0 to 100 and it carries over from day to day. A new Mochi starts at 100. Every night at midnight the day is added up and HP goes into the next day. If HP reaches 0 at midnight, Mochi dies and a baby hatches: the next generation (Mochi #2, #3…), back at 100 HP, wearing your outfit. The goal is to keep one Mochi alive as long as you can, with as few deaths as possible. Home shows its age, generation, your record and your deaths; friends see them on your card.

        How a day is scored

        Every tracked thing is compared with your own target, not a fixed number, so it's fair whether you smoke 2 a day or 20.

        • Things to do less of (cigarettes, alcohol, YouTube minutes…): exactly on target = 0 HP. Under = heals, over = hurts, by how far you are from the target. The most it can give is its full size, the most it can take is 1.5× its size. A target of 0 means quitting: a clean day gives half its size, each one takes half its size.
        • Things to do more of (gym, water, coding…): measured over the last 7 days against your goal, so weekly goals (gym 3× a week) work too. Nothing at all = a small loss (0.4× its size), goal reached = +0.6× its size, a bit more for going past it.
        • Size is how much a thing matters: small 3, medium 6, big 10 HP. You pick it per habit.
        • +2 HP every day just for being alive.
        • Sleep: he learns when you usually go to bed and wake up, and sleeps then. But never while you're still on the computer.
        • Limits: one day can take at most 25 HP and give at most 15. So a healthy Mochi survives at least 4 terrible days in a row.
        • Days with no data at all (computer off, nothing logged) only get the +2.

        During the day

        Home shows HP with today's score so far and a TODAY +/− badge. "Right now" tells you what the next one is worth: the next gym session, glass of water, or what one more cigarette would cost. Below 20 HP Mochi is critical; if today would end at 0, Home warns you that Mochi dies tonight unless you turn the day around.

        Deaths and babies

        When Mochi dies you see how long it lived and what hurt most in its last days. The baby is small for its first days and grows up. Your record (longest life) stays forever.

        Targets

        Set in the last step of the setup and any time in Settings → Targets.

        • Less of: "at most N a day" (minutes for screen time). Filled in with your normal from the last 2 weeks. Leave it empty and Mochi learns it: the habit doesn't count for 7 days, then your average becomes the target.
        • Tighten 10% a week: every week you stay under a target on 5 or more days, it gets about 10% lower (at least 1 lower, or 5 minutes). That slowly walks you down toward quitting. Turn it off per habit if you'd rather lower it yourself.
        • More of: "at least N a day" or "a week".
        • Size: small / medium / big, how much it matters for HP.
        • Screen stats only count for HP if "counts for HP" is ticked (coding, commits and total screen time are on by default).

        Changing a target recalculates today right away. Past days stay as they were.

        Home

        • Mochi in its place (tap it to dress it up), the stat sheet next to it and what Mochi says about your day.
        • Arrows compare with yesterday at the same time: ▲ = more, ▼ = less. Red = worse, green = better (more cigarettes = red ▲, more coding = green ▲).
        • Log buttons for your habits; "More things to log" shows the rest. A wrong tap can be undone from the toast or the Bad habits and Good habits tabs.
        • The ‹ Today › buttons go back to earlier days.

        Habits and logging

        • Built-in habits: cigarettes, joints, vaping, junk food, alcohol, energy drinks, sweets, coffee; gym, walks, water, healthy meals, reading, meditation, stretching.
        • Your own: any name and emoji, "less of" or "more of", and a size.
        • Removing a habit only hides it. Its history stays, and adding it back brings it all back.
        • The Bad habits and Good habits tabs show charts per habit; tap a row for the details of any day.

        Screen time

        Mochi checks which app (and which browser tab) is in front every few seconds and puts it in a category: coding, YouTube, social and chat, streaming, gaming, other websites or other apps. Idle time (no mouse or keyboard) and a locked screen don't count. On Mac it reads the browser tab's address; on Windows it reads the tab's title (Windows doesn't allow more), so common sites are recognized by name. Phone screen time comes from the iPhone (see Your phone).

        Mochi's looks and moods

        • Wardrobe (tap Mochi on Home): looks, body, color, eyes, mouth, head, glasses, neck, outfit, something to hold, and the place behind it. Everything is free. Most items can be any color (the color row and the rainbow picker). Friends see your Mochi on your card.
        • Places: stadium show, rage show, moon base, neon rooftop, volcano, under the sea, cloud kingdom, arcade, and calm ones (sky, bedroom, park, beach) whose sky follows the time of day.
        • Moods follow your day: proud after lots of coding, smoky after more smoking than yesterday, sleepy late at night.
        • States: weed makes it high (red eyes, smoke, swaying, for up to an hour after the last joint), alcohol makes it drunk (more drinks = dizzier), cigarettes make it cough, especially on heavy days.
        • Idle: it breathes, blinks, looks around and hops now and then. To keep everything still, set Settings → Look → Animations to Off (or "Like my computer").

        Smoking detector

        The webcam looks for a cigarette or joint near your mouth, and for the glowing tip when you take a drag (it ignores lamps and RGB lights). After a couple of drags it asks "Cigarette, joint, already registered or neither?" on the computer and on every open Mochi page, phone included. Already registered means you logged it yourself: nothing is logged twice and the camera rests for about 10 minutes. Nothing is recorded: each moment is checked and forgotten, and nothing leaves your computer. Only your answer is logged.

        It's off until you choose it: when you track smoking, Home asks once. It turns off by itself when you're away or the screen is locked, and Home has Pause 1 hour and Pause until tomorrow. The question also tells you when your last cigarette and joint were.

        Settings → Tracking → Smoking detector turns it on or off and shows whether it's watching. If it misses you (weak webcam, colored light), run train.bat / the training to teach it your room. camtest.bat tests the camera on its own.

        Account and friends

        • One account (email, username, password) works on all your devices. Change any of it in Settings → Edit account.
        • Friends: add someone by username. You see each other's card: Mochi, HP, its age and generation, and the stats you choose to share (good stuff is shared by default, bad habits stay private unless you share them). Tap a friend to chat.
        • Only your card and your chat messages go online (to the friends server). Everything else stays on your computer.

        Your phone

        The phone opens your Mochi from the computer through Tailscale (a free private connection between your own devices). In Settings → Your phone: install Tailscale on the computer and the phone with the same account, press Connect my phone, scan the QR code, then Share → Add to Home Screen (Android: ⋮ → Add to Home screen). The computer must be on with Mochi running. It's private: only your own devices on Tailscale can reach it, with a secret code from the QR.

        42 and GitHub

        Optional extras from the setup. 42: blackhole days left, logtime and projects in the Monitoring tab. GitHub: commits count on Home and your commit streak shows on your card.

        Updates

        Mochi updates itself from GitHub: it checks a minute after starting and every few hours, installs the new version and restarts. In Settings → Updates, Check for updates looks for a new version; when one is found, Update now installs it (and checking is off until you do). Your data is never touched by an update.

        Your data

        • Everything lives in one folder on your computer: ~/.lazytrack (on Windows C:\Users\you\.lazytrack): the database lazytrack.db and config.json.
        • Back it up by copying that folder. Deleting your account (Settings) removes only the login; every log stays.
        • Online: only your friend card and chat messages, on the friends server. Camera pictures and screen details never leave the computer.

        How it's built

        • A small Python program runs on your computer: a web server on port 8042 that serves this page, plus background workers (screen tracker, webcam detector, GitHub/42 sync, updater). Data goes into a SQLite database.
        • lazytrack/dashboard.py the web server and API · summary.py daily and weekly stats · life.py HP, targets, deaths and babies · habits.py which habits you track · activity.py screen time · smoke.py the webcam detector · accounts.py login · cloud.py friends · updater.py updates · macos.py / windows.py the system parts.
        • lazytrack/static/dashboard.html is this whole app (Home, tabs, Mochi's pixel art and the wardrobe).
        • Command line: ./lt (Mac) or lt.bat (Windows), e.g. lt log cigarette or lt summary.

        Troubleshooting

        • The page won't load: Mochi isn't running. Open it from the Start menu / Applications (or start.command / start.bat).
        • It opens in the browser instead of its own window: the window part is still being installed after an update; it switches by itself after the next restart.
        • Windows "protected your PC": More info → Run anyway.
        • Camera doesn't work: allow camera access for desktop apps in the system settings, close other apps using it, then check the live view in Settings.
        • Screen time looks low: idle time and the lock screen don't count; on Windows unknown sites count as "Other websites".
        • Friends don't show up: both need to be logged in and online; cards update every couple of minutes.
        • Something else: check.bat on Windows shows what Mochi sees; send a screenshot of the black window.
